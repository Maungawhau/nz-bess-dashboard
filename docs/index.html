What the numbers are

Revenue = arbitrage + reserves, calculated from Electricity Authority EMI data and refreshed every night.

Arbitrage = energy sold (discharge MWh × node price) − energy bought (charge MWh × node price), for each half-hour, at the battery's own node.

Reserves = FIR and SIR megawatts cleared × the island reserve price × 0.5 h. Batteries are paid to stand by; they earn this whether or not a reserve event happens.

What is not included: hedges and contracts, frequency keeping, constrained-on payments, transmission charges, reserve cost allocation, losses, and the cost of building and running the battery. This is gross spot-market revenue, not profit.

Data. Each battery is two dispatch units at its connection point: an injection unit (discharge) and an offtake unit (charging). Discharge comes from cleared generation, charging from dispatched load, and reserves from FIR/SIR cleared on both units. Five-minute dispatch is averaged to half-hours. Prices are final (interim for the most recent days).

Round-trip efficiency is measured from dispatch, so it reads low for batteries that mostly sit on reserve. Their small top-up charging is dispatched, but energy delivered when reserves are triggered is not.

Revenue per MW is annualised: revenue ÷ battery MW × 365 ÷ active days in the period, counted from each battery's first day of activity (so commissioning gaps don't dilute it). Testing periods are included.

Not on the dashboard yet: what the batteries push out of the merit order (gas displacement). A reliable answer needs a constraint-aware re-run of the market model, so it is parked as phase 2.
